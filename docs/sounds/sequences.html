The Sequence Library: 99 of its 1,032 factory sequences

The Sequence Library ships 1,032 sequence presets in 37 styles: drums, bass lines, harmony, pads, arps, melodies and effects, each with a line saying what it does. Here are 99 on their own.

What is shown, and why so few. Most of the library is a smaller set of figures given to many styles on other chords, with another voice and tempo: 315 of the 1,032 sequences are figures only one style has, going by the line that says what each does with its bars, chords and key left out. The risers are the plainest case: 34 of the 38 are one climb up the A minor scale. So a style shows only figures of its own here, each figure once on the whole page, and a part marked Melody, whose notes are always its own. The nine techno and industrial styles show one of each role where they have one of their own; the others show two at most: what the style's name promises where it has it (a drone under Drone, a break under Breaks), a part of its own, a groove of its own, a melody. Under each heading is how many of the style's sequences are its own. An effect plays once; the other parts go round as loops. Each card is marked with what the clip is; the style is only the heading. Chosen by that rule, not by ear: nobody has listened to them one by one, and a figure of a style's own is not proof that it sounds of the style. The kits play six sequences together.

Each clip was rendered by Torqwave's own code (TorqwaveTests --sound-demos, in the repository) and written as an MP3. Nothing was added: no effects, no levelling, no editing. Dead air is cut: a sound slow to arrive (a pad, a drone, a swell, a riser) is joined partway in, 30 ms before it reaches a quarter of its peak, with a fade in, and its card says how far in; silence after a sound has died away is left off; and a clip still sounding where it stops is faded over its last 30 ms so the cut does not click. Each plays on the voices it comes with, Torqwave Synth patches and Basic Drums kits, at the tempo it was written at, as the Sequence Library's own preview plays it; a loop under five seconds goes round again. 11.6 MB in all.
