# Styles

Every factory preset in the [Sequence Library](07-instruments-and-midi.md#presets) carries a style, and it is written to that style's conventions. This page says what those conventions are, so the presets can teach as well as play: open one in the [Sequencer](07-instruments-and-midi.md#the-sequencer) next to the row here and you can see each rule in its notes. **Filters > Style** in the library shows a style's presets, and **By Style** groups them (see [Sort, group and filter](07-instruments-and-midi.md#sort-group-and-filter)).

The presets lean towards driving and peak-time techno, dark and industrial techno and EBM. Melodic, minimal and the rest have smaller sets.

## Techno

| Style | Tempo | What defines it |
|---|---|---|
| Techno › Dark | 128–135 | A short kick with a long rumble on the 16ths after it, offbeat hats under 70 % velocity, a ride in 8ths with every second hit quieter, a rolling bass on the root, one-note minor stabs |
| Techno › Driving | 130–138 | Kick-bass-bass-bass rolling 16ths, a clap on 2 and 4 layered with a snare, 16th hats shaped by velocity, ghost kicks before 2 and 4 |
| Techno › Peak-time | 130–138 | The driving groove at full weight: call-and-response riffs, stabs and builds for the top of a set |
| Techno › Percussive | 132–142 | Loop tracks built from percussion: tom, conga and metal figures in 16ths with shifting accents, a rolling kick and bass, little or no harmony |
| Techno › Hypnotic | 128–134 | Percussion in 3- and 5-step cycles against the bar, very few pitches, 4- and 8-bar loops that change slowly |
| Techno › Minimal | 122–130 | Sparse clicks and rims, a short syncopated bass, one dub chord |
| Techno › Club tools | 125–132 | Kick only, kick and hat, top loops and percussion loops: parts built for layering |
| Techno › Melodic | 120–126 | A rolling 16th bass on the chord roots, i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII and i–iv–♭VI–♭VII progressions, 16th arps over the chords, a 1-bar lead restated over 8 |
| Techno › Industrial | 126–130 | An overdriven kick, low tom figures in the kick's gaps, a noise snare, metallic percussion |
| Techno › Hard | 145–155 | An offbeat rumble, metallic 16th percussion with accents every 3 and 6 steps, kick rolls |
| Techno › Acid | 128–140 | 303 lines: 16 steps, the root with octave jumps, rests, accents on a quarter to two fifths of the steps, slides on a tenth to a quarter, minor pentatonic or Phrygian |

## Industrial

| Style | Tempo | What defines it |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial › EBM | 110–130 | A sequenced 16th or 8th bass in the kick's gaps, the jump from low to octave, a short gate and rests so it breathes; roots moving i to ♭VI to ♭VII or i to ♭II; disco or backbeat drums with near-flat "machine" velocities and big gated snares; alarm lines a semitone or a tritone apart |
| Industrial › Electro-industrial | 100–135 | Syncopated kicks, flammed snares, a 16th bass with a semitone shift, Phrygian and harmonic minor clusters, pitch-bend dives |
| Industrial › Industrial rock | 90–140 | Programmed rock beats, synth riffs doubling a guitar in 8ths with ♭2 and ♭5 moves, power chords |
| Industrial › Futurepop | 125–140 | Trance drums and an offbeat bass, minor 4-chord loops, gated 16th arps, 2-bar call-and-answer leads |
| Industrial › Aggrotech | 130–150 | A hard 4/4, snare-roll fills, a distorted offbeat bass, 16th supersaw riffs |
| Industrial › Synth-EBM | 120–135 | EBM bass sequences under synth-pop chords and a sung hook: 8th and 16th bass on the roots, minor progressions, square and saw leads in 2-bar phrases |
| Industrial › Darkwave | 100–118 | Drum-machine pop beats with long-reverb snares, an 8th root bass, chorused minor pads, simple 2-bar melodies |
| Industrial › Techno-body | 125–140 | A techno kick and hats with an EBM 16th bass in the gaps, metallic percussion, the root changing in bar 3 or 4 |

Front 242 popularised the name "Electronic Body Music" with their 1984 *No Comment* EP. The term was used earlier: Ralf Hütter spoke of body music in 1977–78, and DAF of "Körpermusik" in 1981.

## What every preset keeps

- **On the 16th grid**, 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars long. A 4-bar preset changes in its last bar: a fill, an answer or a root move.
- **In A minor.** A1 is 55 Hz, which sits well in the sub, and every preset stacks with every other. Some use Phrygian (B♭ in place of B) or harmonic minor (G♯ in place of G), and say so in their tags. **Filters > Transpose Drops to the Session's Key** moves them into the session's key as you drop them.
- **Drums on the General MIDI map:** kick 36, rim 37, snare 38, clap 39, closed hat 42, open hat 46, toms 41, 45 and 48, crash 49, ride 51, shaker 70. They play any drum plug-in or kit that follows it.
- **Bass one note at a time**, apart from an acid slide, which overlaps the next note by a hair so the synth glides.
- **Velocities carry the groove:**

  | Hit | Velocity |
  |---|---|
  | Accent | 110–127 |
  | Normal | 90–105 |
  | Weak hat | 60–80 |
  | Ghost | 25–50 |

- **Written at the style's usual tempo.** A sequence plays at the session's tempo wherever it goes, so this is only where it was written.
- **Original.** Each pattern is written to its style's conventions, not copied from a record, and named for what it is ("Belgian Body Bass", "Rumble Roller"), never for a band or a song.
