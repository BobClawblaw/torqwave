Torqwave Synth · part three · step 16

Three looks for the signal console

Each picture shows the same patch, the same twelve sounds within reach and the same route. Switch each one between Trace (moving through sounds by ear) and Decrypt (cracking a recording into a patch). These are drawings to choose from, not the built window.

A

Orbit

The patch you hold in the middle, twelve nodes in a ring, links between. The simplest to read at a glance.

  • Position means nothing. Every node sits at the same distance, in key order from C3 to B3, so it maps straight onto an octave of keys.
  • Reach is the ring. Turn Reach up and the ring widens.
  • Trade-off: the calmest of the three, and the one that tells you least before you listen.
B

Lattice

A hex grid with a live log beside it. Inner cells are near, outer cells are far. The most terminal-like.

  • Two distances at once. Six near cells and six far ones, so one screen offers small and large moves.
  • The log is the route. Every jump, firewall and typed request is a line you can click to return to.
  • Trade-off: the densest; the log takes a third of the width from the map.
C

Sweep

A scope. The direction of a node says which part of the sound changed; its distance says how much.

  • You can aim. Want a different filter and the same oscillators? Look in the filter sector. A firewall closes a sector off.
  • In Decrypt, the middle is the signal. Candidates drift inward as their lock rises; the closest is the best match.
  • Trade-off: the most to learn in the first minute, and the most it tells you after that.

My pick is C, Sweep. It is the only one where the picture carries information the other two leave to your ears, and Decrypt reads the same way as Trace. A is the safe choice; B has the strongest hacker feel but the least room.
