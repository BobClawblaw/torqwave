# Roadmap

What comes next, in order. Finished work moves to the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md).

**Direction: an editor and a DAW in one.** Torqwave is becoming a hybrid: the audio editor, repair and mastering suite it already is, and a place to design sounds, to write and produce music and to work to picture, with the editor's tools reaching into every clip of a session. The aim is not another copy of the big DAWs, but the things only a program built this way can do. The multitrack view already has instrument tracks with note clips, the Piano Roll and MIDI recording (milestones 362 to 367), a session tempo and time signature, a loop, a bar grid with snapping, a Track Inspector and Ask Torq working on sessions. 0.19.0 added comping across takes, audio clips that follow the tempo, an arranger for whole sections, automation modes and VCA faders, MIDI out and a drum grid, control surfaces, working to picture beyond the video panel, and the Sequence Library, Sequencer and Idea Board. 0.20.0 to 0.22.1 added built-in instruments worth playing (Torqwave Synth with 9 oscillators, Decrypt and 116 Complex club patches, Basic Drums with 64 pads / 54 sources / 16-layer sampling and Battery 4 import, StringSwarm and Torqwave Sampler) and the rewritten Sequence Library (1,024 unique sequences and 116 kits). 0.23.0 added six Live Creative FX, four Live MIDI FX, Torqwave Synth LFOs 3–4 / 24 mod slots / Slot Depth / MSEG Curve / built-in Arpeggiator / 4-scene Evolve morphing, Piano Roll ratchets / micro-timing / groove templates / ghost notes, Audio to MIDI (melody and drums), Torqwave Sampler SFZ import / round-robin groups / tempo-synced Warp (still held in memory, no disk streaming or SF2), Basic Drums 8-bus multi-out, Track-Level Modulators, native CLAP 1.2 hosting, and headless CI tests on Windows and Linux.

What real use turns up goes into the [fix log](docs/DOGFOODING.md) and its [issues](https://github.com/BobClawblaw/torqwave/issues), and is fixed alongside the new work. 1.0.0 follows once the hybrid has had a round of real use, designing sounds, writing music and working to picture, with nothing left in the log. The items below wait behind that.

**Done, not on this list:** the mastering suite and the audio-repair suite are in (milestones 297 to 326: every job the big suites do, built our own way, listed in the changelog), as are the Sound Library, surround, video, projects, batch processing, the stretch methods and the rest. What is left of them is use: nothing from milestones 297 to 326 has been listened to yet, and each of those tools has been run once on four real recordings and no more. Open from those runs: a room full of voices is noise to Adaptive Noise Reduction and Dialogue Isolation, which need quiet moments or one pitch (both say so when they find almost nothing to keep); and a rough character voice reads as voiced in 46 % of its loud frames at the middle sensitivity, the rest having two harmonics over the floor or periods unlike each other (taking two harmonics for a voice took hum for a voice too, and was dropped). Both go to the fix log if real use runs into them.

1. **Sound Library category suggestions:** most libraries don't name their files the UCS way, so a category could be suggested for the rest (never taken as the sound's category until accepted). Not built, because what has been tried isn't good enough to offer. Measured on one large library, each vendor's UCS-named sounds guessed from the others': scoring UCS's own words and synonyms against a sound's name and folders got the category right for 0 to 63% of most vendors' sounds; learning from the library's own UCS-named sounds as well (naive Bayes over name and folder words, UCS's words as the prior) got 40% of categories and 17% of exact subcategories, and its confidence didn't single out the right ones (44% right among the half it was surest of). The sounds whose names follow UCS come from only six vendors, 85% of them from one (mostly footsteps), so there is little to learn from. Suggesting from a folder's other sounds was measured too and reaches almost nothing that is really UCS: of the 646,200 sounds there without a category, 31,154 share a folder with sounds that have one, and nearly all of those folders' categorised sounds are ordinary all-caps names that happen to start with a general CatID (WIND_LTL2-0988, RAIN_LIGHT_TO_HEAVY_CS); the folders named the UCS way are named so throughout. Such all-caps names can't be told from UCS names by their shape (requiring UCS's four fields would drop 108 real UCS names, ROBTVox_Mechanimal 070_F0X among them, and keep about as many ordinary ones), so about 150 of that library's sounds still take WIND, RAIN or BEEPS from their names. Next to try: words from the audio itself.

2. **A Developer ID** for notarizing, the remaining step before installing opens without Privacy & Security.

3. **AI assistance with a model you run yourself (experimental, in since 0.17.0, on files, sessions and sequences):** an optional language model, off by default, on any server that speaks the OpenAI-compatible API (a local model through Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM and the like, on your computer or network), turning sentences into plans of Torqwave's commands, plug-in chains and settings (built-in and third-party AU, CLAP, VST3 and LV2), batch jobs, names, Sound Library categories and MIDI, each shown before it is applied and undone in one step. Torqwave ships no model, and sends no audio unless Let the model hear clips is turned on for Decrypt. Models that work on audio (transcription, polyphonic audio to MIDI beyond 0.23.0's DSP melody/drum extraction, generation) come after it. Stem separation with a model was looked at and is out of scope ([the record](docs/plans/PLAN-STEM-SEPARATION.md)). The plan and the tools the model can use are in [AI assistance](docs/AI.md).
